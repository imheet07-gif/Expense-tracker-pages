Expense Tracker Copy Page

Code for your spending screen and for the card-alerts sync. Use the buttons to copy; you don't need to read the code.

New: import statements, merchant categories, and a year-to-date view (7 Oct)

  • Import statements: Expenses tab › Import statements (PDF). Pick one or more bank or credit card statements (password-protected ones ask once). Each spend you didn't log is added to Notion. Spends you already logged (by hand, the shortcut or auto-log) just get their card filled in. Ones waiting in your alert inbox are linked to the alert, so it stops asking. Importing the same statement again adds nothing. Card payments, refunds, incoming money, cash withdrawals and investments (IPO, SIP, broker) aren't counted as spending.
  • Big bank transfers (₹1 lakh or more): after the rest is saved, you're asked about each one: It was spending, Spending, but a one-off (saved under "Big one-offs", outside your monthly budget), or Just moving money (skipped, and not asked again). Skip all for now asks again next time you import that statement. Card spends and debit card purchases of any size are always added.
  • Taking an import back: Settings › Imported statements lists each import with Remove (tap twice). It deletes only the expenses that import added (they go to Notion's Trash for 30 days). Expenses you'd logged yourself stay, with the card it filled in cleared, and alerts it used go back to your inbox. Imports from before this update aren't listed.
  • Learns from your logging: each imported spend gets the category (and the name) you've used for that merchant before, or else what the merchant's name suggests.
  • Merchant categories: on any expense, Edit › Category. With "Always put … here" ticked, that merchant always goes there, its earlier expenses move too, and imports use it. Listed in Settings › Merchant categories.
  • Auto-log preview, clearer: payments less than an hour old now show as "waiting" (in "Fill in what I miss" they're added only after an hour, if you haven't logged them), instead of "Nothing would have been added". If To do has unlogged payments from before auto-log started, the preview says those won't be added for you.
  • Year to date: the top of the Trends tab shows what you've spent this year: the total, the monthly average, the change against the same months last year, each category's share (tap one to see its expenses), and where most of it went. It follows the Year summary's calendar year or financial year switch. The Expenses tab also has a year filter now.

The PDF is read on your iPhone; only the expenses go to Notion. The first import adds an Import ID column to your Expenses table. For a full year, import a few months at a time: each spend takes about half a second to save. Paste the new Expenses Dashboard (version 6.8). If you use the setup link, also paste it into the link's dashboard file and deploy a new version, so friends get it. The private edition has all of this too: paste it again.

New: a Loans tab, interest vs principal, rate changes and term (4 Oct, late)

Loans tab. Loans now have their own tab next to Events. The top shows what you pay in EMIs every month, what's still to repay, this year's interest and principal, a month-by-month chart for all loans together (Bajaj until September, HDFC from October), and what was paid in the month you're viewing. Each loan has its own chart: orange is interest, blue is principal, faded bars are estimates; tap a bar for that month's split. The Overview keeps a one-line Loans summary that opens the tab.

Rate changes. Each rate is kept with the date it applies from, so earlier months keep the old rate and this year's interest stays right for tax time. When your lender emails a new rate, the sync adds it from the date in the email and emails you once. If you change it yourself, tap Rate changed, or update outstanding, enter the new rate and the date it applies from.

When the lender changes the EMI instead (as Bajaj did when rates fell): the new EMI is used from the month it's debited, and the card works out the rate it implies if the term stayed the same, e.g. "EMI went down from ₹53,783 to ₹52,900 on 2 Sep … your rate went from 8.75% to about 8.47%", with a Use 8.47% button that adds it to the rate history. If a prepayment came just before, it says so instead, since the EMI may have dropped because of the prepayment.
